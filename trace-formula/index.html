The Trace Formula Project

A multimedia reference book series on trace formulas, bringing together mathematical exposition, Lean formalizations, and video explanations in one place.

Open the website Volume 1a PDF Source on GitHub

The idea

Trace formulas sit at the center of modern analytic number theory. The Petersson and Kuznetsov formulas, the Selberg trace formula, and their relative versions are the tools behind results on moments of L-functions, subconvexity, equidistribution, and non-vanishing. Almost every paper I have written uses one.

Yet learning them is hard. The ideas are spread across research papers and a handful of books, each with its own normalizations, and the pictures that make the subject intuitive rarely make it to the page. This project is my attempt to fix that by developing the material through three connected perspectives, in three formats at once.

  • A book. The natural-language text, written in LaTeX with complete arguments and consistent conventions, and built as a PDF.
  • A website. The same manuscript presented alongside its Lean sources, with Read, Watch, Formalization, Compare, and Map views for every section.
  • A video series. Chapter lectures on Screech Math, built from the same source as the text.

The series

Each volume treats one setting, and each has three parts: A, automorphic forms; B, automorphic representations; and C, the dictionary between them.

  • Volume 1 · The modular group SL₂(ℤ)1a in draft
  • Volume 2 · Congruence subgroups of SL₂(ℤ), including nebentypusPlanned
  • Volume 3 · Half-integral weightPlanned
  • Volume 4 · Quaternionic formsPlanned

Every theorem in the book is paired with a Lean formalization, and the Compare view on the website puts the prose statement and its formal counterpart side by side. The formalizations build with no admitted proofs, but machine verification is separate from checking that the formal statement says what the prose says. That correspondence is reviewed by hand, and the videos are part of that review. See the Lean page for more on why.

Status

The series is under active development. Volume 1a has a full outline of 31 chapters and nine appendices, and Chapter 0 (traces, integral kernels, and weighted diagonal traces) has its manuscript, Lean companions, and a draft lecture script. Most chapters remain unwritten. The website rebuilds automatically as material lands, so the live site is always current.
